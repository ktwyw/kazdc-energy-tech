The plant and its inputs

Both arrangements deliver coolant at the same supply temperature, Ts, to IT equipment releasing a steady sensible heat load, Q. The coolant absorbs that heat and returns to the plant at Tr. In the baseline a chiller removes all the heat. In the alternative the return coolant passes first through an outdoor dry cooler, then through a chiller that supplies any remaining cooling.

The subscripts th and e distinguish heat duty from electrical input. The only thermal load in the loop balance is the IT heat. The electrical comparison counts the cooling plant. Rack electricity, room air handlers, UPS losses and other building loads fall outside this boundary.

The weather year

The weather references document the file and its source flags. The input is the Climate.OneBuilding TMYx file for Astana, Kazakhstan, station 351880. A typical meteorological year joins representative months drawn from 2011–2025. The file’s outdoor dry-bulb temperatures range from $low to $high °C. Dry bulb is the relevant air temperature because this model transfers sensible heat to air through a dry coil.

The reader takes the dry-bulb field from each hourly EnergyPlus weather (EPW) record. It preserves the source year, month, day and hour index (1–24), and assigns one hour to each row. The EPW header specifies UTC+6 local standard time and the records carry minute 30. Month totals follow those source labels. Kazakhstan now uses UTC+5. Only the daily tank controller converts source time to the current civil clock for its operating schedule. The reader checks calendar order and coverage and rejects duplicate hours, missing values and invalid temperatures.

In the Kazakhstan comparison, each city contributes its own 8,760-hour file to the same plant calculation. The city selector gives the location and years available to the weather compiler. The 2011–2025 collection includes a shorter record at Taraz Airport (10 source years within 2011–2021) and 14 source years at Aktau. Ekibastuz, Qonaev and Taldykorgan use temperatures reconstructed by ERA5. The regional results link to the original file for each city.

Dry-cooler capacity

The rated approach fixes one known point on the cooler’s performance curve. When the fan runs continuously and the dry cooler alone carries the design load, the coolant leaves at Ts and the entering outdoor air is A degrees cooler. The driving difference from entering coolant to entering air is then ΔT + A = $design_diff K.

Full-fan capacity at outdoor temperature Ta

G = Q / (ΔT + A)

Qcap = G × max(0, Tr − Ta)

For the reference plant, G = $load / $design_diff = $coefficient kW/K. This is an effective coefficient fitted at one rating point. A detailed exchanger model would resolve the two streams and their heat-transfer characteristics. Full-fan capacity can exceed the IT load in cold air. Useful cooling is capped at the load.

At fixed flow, the coolant heat-capacity rate is Cw = Q/ΔT = $capacity_rate kW/K. If the dry cooler removes Qd, its hourly mean outlet temperature is Tr − Qd/Cw. The chiller removes the remaining heat to reach the supply setpoint. This gives a second way to check the heat split.

When the fan runs

The chiller-only plant always draws Pbase = Pp + Q/COP = $pump + $base_chiller = $base_kw kWe. The controller compares that demand with the candidate using outdoor cooling.

Positive dry-cooler capacity

Qd,cand = min(Q, Qcap),  d = Qd,cand / Qcap

Pcand = Pp + dF + (Q − Qd,cand) / COP

The fan duty fraction d is the fraction of the hour at full fan power. In cold weather the model cycles the fan and assumes the buffered loop meets the hourly load. During assisted operation the fan runs for the whole hour. The pump runs in either case.

Whenever capacity is positive, the saving offered by the candidate is d(Qcap/COP − F). The fan therefore runs when Qcap > COP × F. The controller bypasses at equality or zero capacity. On bypass, dry heat and fan power both become zero.

Full-load temperature

$full_c °C outdoors

Set Qcap = Q. The physical boundary is Ts − A. At that point the fan can carry the whole load continuously.

Electrical break-even

$bypass_c °C outdoors

Set Qcap = COP × F = $break_heat kWth. The corresponding outdoor temperature is Tr − COP × F/G.

From hours to an annual result

For a month or year, add each hourly average power times one hour and divide by 1,000 to convert kWh to MWh. Count each hourly record in exactly one operating mode. Compute each annual percentage from annual totals, which weights the months by their hours and energy.

Period electricity and saving

E [MWh] = Σh Ph [kW] × 1 h / 1,000

Saving [%] = 100 × (Ebase − Ehybrid) / Ebase

July provides a monthly check. The $july_hours hours comprise $july_full chiller-off, $july_assist assisted and $july_bypass chiller-only hours. The baseline uses $july_base MWh of electricity and the hybrid uses $july_hybrid MWh, a $july_saved% saving.

Full outdoor cooling supplies $off_heat MWhth. The $assisted assisted hours supply another $assisted_heat MWhth through the dry cooler. The chiller supplies $chiller_heat MWhth over the year. These heat flows account for the entire IT load.

On the electrical side, the hybrid uses $chiller_mwh MWh for the chiller, $fan_mwh MWh for the dry-cooler fan and $pump_mwh MWh for the pump, totalling $hybrid_mwh MWh before rounding the components. The pump consumes the same electricity in the baseline and therefore cancels from the absolute saving. It is included in both annual totals and in the percentage denominator.

Calibrating the equipment. Use a rated dry-cooler performance point at the intended coolant flow and glycol concentration, the actual fan control curve, pump measurements, and chiller COP versus ambient temperature, coolant setpoint and part load. The reference COP stays fixed, which is why the baseline is flat across the supply-temperature sweep. Varying it_kw in this model also resizes the dry cooler through G.

Checking operation. Compare predicted mode hours and plant electricity with metered data for the same weather, loads and loop temperatures. Freeze-protection heaters, cycling constraints, fouling, pump heat and distribution losses can matter at a real site. Multi-year series and design extremes address year-to-year variation and equipment sizing. The typical-year result estimates operating energy under the stated assumptions.

Reproduce the calculation

Extract the project ZIP. {{SOURCE_LINK}} From its root, run the following commands with Python 3.10 or later and the bundled weather CSV. The calculation, tests and page builder use the Python standard library.

python cooling.py --supply $supply --output results_reference
python -m unittest discover -s tests -v
python build.py

The browser studies also have a JavaScript test suite. After building the pages, run node --test with Node.js 18 or later. The tests compare hourly cooling to the city results and check energy balances, storage constraints and heat-pump limits.

In results_reference/hourly.csv, verify that dry_kw_th + chiller_kw_th equals the IT load in every row. Sum hybrid_kw_e and divide by 1,000 to recover annual hybrid MWh. Count rows with mode equal to dry_only to recover chiller-off hours. The supplied benchmarks are in reference/monthly.csv and reference/annual.csv.

The tests check the cold-hour fan fraction, partial-load heat balance, tie at break-even, calendar completeness and mode counts across the 49 dashboard supply settings. They verify implementation against the stated model. Physical validation would require equipment and site measurements.
