Methods

Measure both sides of the UPS

Electrical loss (kW) = IT output (kW) × (1 / efficiency − 1)

Use efficiency as a fraction in the equation. Multiplying loss by 8,760 hours gives annual kWh. The reference and alternative supply identical IT output, so the difference in input electricity is the difference in their losses. A negative saving means the alternative draws more electricity.

At 1 MW delivered, an increase from 94% to 97% reduces conversion losses by about 33 kW. Where the UPS is installed determines how much of that heat enters a cooled room. The calculator counts only the electricity measured across the UPS. Battery discharge, recharge, redundancy switching and any extra cooling require separate operating data.

Try a load-and-meter demonstration

For each UPS, measure input and output simultaneously at the same stable output power. Efficiency is output divided by input. If the two units have been tested at different loads, use their measured efficiency curves to select values at a common output before comparing annual losses. Electric power at part load can differ from a nameplate rating.
