Direct exchange or temperature lift

Direct supply requires the hot rack return to exceed the user’s supply temperature by at least 3 K. For this screen the user return is 10 K below its supply, the rack loop cools by 6 K at full capture, and flows can be controlled. At that threshold the cold-end approach is at least 7 K. An exchanger sized for the captured duty is assumed. A detailed design needs both measured flows, temperatures and a vendor performance curve.

When direct transfer qualifies, delivered heat is the smaller of building demand and available rack heat. Otherwise direct delivery is zero. With heat-pump backup enabled, the model adds compressor work until demand is met or the source heat is exhausted. The heating COP is constant at the selected operating point. In heat-pump mode, source flow is modulated to maintain a 6 K water temperature drop while heat is extracted. The coldest source water therefore leaves at the rack return temperature minus 6 K. For a single evaporating and condensing temperature, the optimistic reversible heating COP is Tuser supply,K / (Tuser supply,K − Tleaving source,K). Both temperatures are absolute. For a 15 °C rack return and 70 °C delivery, the source leaves at 9 °C and the bound is 343.15 / 61 ≈ 5.63. A real heat pump must operate below this bound, and its performance map sets the achievable COP.

Heat delivered = heat taken from the rack + compressor electricity

Remaining heating demand is the building demand minus the heat delivered by the rack and heat pump. The building’s other heating equipment is outside the calculation. Loop pumping, pipe losses, simultaneous residual cooling and displaced boiler energy are outside this heat balance. The thermal output is a separate service from cooling-plant electricity.
