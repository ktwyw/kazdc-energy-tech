Teaching and testing

From a prediction to a test

Before the run

Define the test section around the heater and the two water-temperature sensors. Record a prediction before taking readings. Note the flow-meter and probe uncertainties. Check the two probes side by side at the same temperature to estimate their relative offset.

Collect one matched interval

Use the same time window for flow, inlet and outlet readings, and the heater’s energy meter. Enter mean values once flow and temperatures have settled. With appreciable fluctuations, divide the run into shorter steady intervals and sum their energies. Record the pump’s meter separately if its heat enters the test section.

Water duty = mass flow × 4.18 kJ/(kg·K) × (outlet − inlet)

For the example, 0.100 kg/s through a 5.0 K rise carries 2.09 kW. Over half an hour this gives 1.045 kWh. The 4.18 value approximates liquid water’s specific heat. A glycol loop needs its own heat capacity at the measured concentration and temperature.

Explain the residual

The signed difference includes heat exchanged with the surroundings, energy stored while the rig warms, and meter uncertainty. A positive result alone cannot distinguish them. Repeat at a second flow rate with the same heater power and compare the two balances. A smaller temperature rise makes probe offsets more important.
