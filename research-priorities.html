Research priorities for Kazakhstan

Which cooling designs merit a closer look in Kazakhstan? One operating data centre could help test the regional model and show which inputs matter most for the next design decision.

Download the one-page meeting brief (PDF)

Three questions for the next studies

How closely does the model match an operating data centre?

What to study

Match actual weather, IT load and coolant temperatures to the cooling electricity meter and the chiller’s on/off record. Set aside complete weeks before calibrating the model.

What the result would tell us

An operator could see the error in predicted electricity use and chiller-off hours on weeks the model has never seen.

How much backup cooling is needed in the hottest hours?

What to study

Run several years of hourly weather for selected cities using equipment performance curves and the facility’s coolant limits.

What the result would tell us

A designer could compare annual electricity savings with the peak backup cooling duty in kW of heat, including the hours that set the peak.

Where could a building use heat from a data centre?

What to study

Match hourly heat available from the data centre with one nearby building or heat network. Check the receiving temperature, distribution losses and electricity for pumps and any heat pump.

What the result would tell us

A building operator could see how much heat reaches the user each month and how much additional electricity it takes to deliver it.

A first study with one operator

This example scope assumes that a partner can supply historical operating records. The timetable and effort can be refined together.

Download the one-page meeting brief (PDF)
Data partner
One willing data-centre operator in Kazakhstan, with an operations engineer who knows the meters and controls.
Hourly records
Use at least eight weeks of time-aligned, anonymised records. Include IT load, outdoor temperature, supply and return temperatures, cooling-plant electricity and chiller status.
Proposed timetable
In weeks 1–2, check meters and reserve the last two weeks of records. In weeks 3–5, calibrate on the earlier records. In weeks 6–8, test on the reserved weeks and review the errors with the operator.
Staff time
Allow 12 analyst-days and 3 operator-engineer days across the eight-week study. These are planning assumptions.
Metering costs
Additional meter hire is 0 KZT if existing meters cover all channels. For missing readings, price rental days × the local daily rate, plus installation and calibration. Confirm a KZT quote with the operator before fieldwork.
First deliverable
One anonymised reference case reporting predicted and metered cooling electricity (kWh), chiller-off hours and errors for the reserved weeks.
